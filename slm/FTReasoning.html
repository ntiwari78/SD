Fine-Tuning LLMs for Reasoning & Chain-of-Thought

A focused companion to the general fine-tuning guide — covering the supervised, reinforced, and self-corrective paradigms that turn next-token predictors into deliberate, multi-step thinkers.

CoT-SFT · DCoT · Select2Reason RLHF · GRPO · DPO · STaR DeepSeek-R1 · Qwen3-Next · MoE reasoning Updated 6 May 2026

1. Introduction: Why Reasoning Matters #

LLMs trained purely on next-token prediction excel at fluent text but often struggle with logical and multi-step problem solving. Research has shown that asking models to generate intermediate chains of thought (CoTs) improves accuracy on complex tasks. The idea was popularized by Google researchers in 2022 who demonstrated that prompting LLMs with "Let's think step by step" encourages them to reason out loud. Subsequent work introduced chain-of-thought fine-tuning: models are further trained on datasets containing problem statements, intermediate reasoning traces, and final answers — so the model emulates human-like reasoning rather than merely pattern-matching answers.

Unlike generic fine-tuning, reasoning fine-tuning has to deal with four distinct properties:

  1. Learning to think before answering. The model must generate a plausible sequence of reasoning steps before its final answer.
  2. Faithfulness and error accumulation. The reasoning trace should actually lead to the answer; errors early in the chain propagate.
  3. Data scarcity. Annotating high-quality reasoning traces is expensive, so automatic methods or distillation from larger models are common.
  4. Long-context management. Reasoning often spans long token chains, motivating sparse-attention and Mixture-of-Experts architectures.

The Six Thinking Hats lens. The recent survey "Putting on the Thinking Hats" (Chen et al., 2026) categorises CoT fine-tuning along six dimensions, mapping each to one of Edward de Bono's metaphorical "thinking hats":

Bluedetailed planning  ·  Greendivergent thinking  ·  Redintuitive judgment  ·  Blacktimely reflection  ·  Yellowinternal reasoning  ·  Whitefact perception

Reasoning fine-tuning, the survey argues, "leverages both supervised and reinforced approaches to cultivate skills like detailed planning, divergent thinking, and fact perception." This document follows the same structural arc.

2. Foundations: Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) for Reasoning #

Pre-training vs. fine-tuning vs. reasoning fine-tuning

Pre-training is unsupervised next-token modeling on massive corpora. Fine-tuning adapts a pre-trained model to a narrower distribution using labelled data and/or feedback. Reasoning fine-tuning typically occurs after instruction tuning: the model already knows to follow instructions and now must learn to think while doing so.

Chen et al. describe transforming a Reflex Model (instinctive answerer) into a Thinking Model (deliberate reasoner) using two stages: supervised fine-tuning (SFT) followed by reinforced fine-tuning (RFT).

2.1 The Three SFT Paradigms

ParadigmWhat the model outputsStrengthsLimitations
Pre-thinking CoT first → answer last. Cross-entropy is applied over the concatenation of question + reasoning + answer. Enables problem decomposition; widely adopted. Suffers from unfaithfulness (reasoning trace doesn't lead to the final answer) and error accumulation (early mistakes propagate).
Post-thinking Answer first → explanation after. Faster response; mitigates error accumulation because the answer precedes the reasoning. Lacks problem-decomposition ability; suitable mainly for smaller models on simple tasks.
Multi-task learning Prefix tokens indicate whether to generate full reasoning or answer-only. Improves training stability and inference speed. Separates reasoning from answer generation, which can increase unfaithfulness.

2.2 Acquiring Chain-of-Thought Data

SFT relies on high-quality reasoning traces. The "Thinking Hats" survey identifies two acquisition routes:

  • Manual annotation. Either implicit — annotators highlight supporting spans (e-SNLI, MultiRC, FEVER) — or explicit — annotators write out reasoning steps (used in GSM8K and MATH). Manual CoTs are high-fidelity but expensive and domain-specific.
  • Automatic annotation. A strong reasoning model (e.g., DeepSeek-R1 or GPT-4) generates reasoning traces for smaller models. Rule-based methods derive CoTs from structured data — e.g., converting medical knowledge graphs into reasoning paths. Both require post-processing and verification, often using answer verifiers or external checkers to filter incorrect traces.

The reasoning data record format

According to Together AI's reasoning fine-tuning documentation, training data should split the chain-of-thought into a dedicated reasoning field and the final response into a content field. Crucially, fine-tuning a reasoning model without reasoning data can actually degrade reasoning ability:

{
  "prompt":    "Question or instruction",
  "reasoning": "Step-by-step chain of thought",
  "content":   "Final answer"
}

3. Reinforced Fine-Tuning (RFT) #

After SFT, reinforced fine-tuning further improves reasoning by optimizing a reward signal. Chen et al. group RFT methods into four categories with explicit mathematical formulations.

3.1 PPO-like (RLHF)

Combines a reward model and a value model. A reward model is trained from human preference pairs; PPO then updates the actor model while penalizing divergence (KL) from the pre-tuned reference model. RLHF improves reasoning but requires four models in memory (actor, reference, reward, value) and suffers from instability and heavy resource use. See HF's "Illustrating RLHF" for the canonical primer.

3.2 GRPO-like

Originating in DeepSeek-Math and central to DeepSeek-R1, Group Relative Policy Optimization estimates advantage by comparing multiple outputs for the same input — eliminating the value model entirely.

Ai = ( ri − mean(r1…G) ) / std(r1…G)

Variants address known weaknesses: DAPO and GSPO tackle entropy collapse and stability; DRA-GRPO introduces diversity rewards. For deeper math see "Understanding the Math Behind GRPO" and "Why GRPO is Important and How it Works".

3.3 DPO-like

Direct Preference Optimization treats the preference model as part of the objective: it directly optimizes the policy to prefer the better answer over the worse one and removes the explicit reward model. Follow-ups refine the granularity (Token-level DPO) or eliminate even the reference model (CPO). See arXiv:2305.18290 — DPO.

3.4 STaR-like (Self-Taught with Reasoning)

Self-training variants such as STaR collect multiple reasoning trajectories from the model itself and fine-tune only on the trajectories that match the correct answer. The model is encouraged to explore; incorrect traces are discarded. This produces a tight loop: generate → verify → train on what worked.

3.5 Reward Modeling

RFT relies on preference data and reward modeling. Two axes of design:

  • Outcome-based rewards evaluate only the final answer — easy to compute but blind to bad reasoning paths that happen to land on the right answer.
  • Process-based rewards evaluate intermediate reasoning steps, assigning credit to each step. More aligned but expensive to annotate.

Specialized discriminative or generative reward models (GenRM-CoT) can be trained to score reasoning, but they need annotated preference data. Rule-based methods assign reward 1 if the answer matches the reference and 0 otherwise — simple and effective for math and coding. LLM-based evaluations ask a strong LLM to critique or verify the reasoning itself.

4. Advanced Techniques #

4.1 Divergent Chain-of-Thought (DCoT)

DCoT (ICLR 2025) requires the model to generate multiple reasoning chains before selecting an answer. Instruction tuning on DCoT datasets "consistently improves performance over the CoT baseline across model families and scales (1.3 B – 70 B)." The improvement comes from self-correction: the model compares divergent reasoning paths and chooses the best answer. DCoT helps even smaller models approach the reasoning performance of much larger ones. Full paper: arXiv:2407.03181.

4.2 Select2Reason: Efficient Long-CoT Data Selection

Fine-tuning on large synthetic instruction sets (200 k+ examples) is expensive. Select2Reason (arXiv:2505.17266) proposes a data-selection strategy that prioritizes instructions which elicit long chains of thought. It uses a quantifier to estimate question difficulty and a reasoning-trace-length heuristic, combining them through a weighted scheme to rank examples.

On OpenR1-Math-220k, fine-tuning on only 10 % of the selected data achieves performance competitive with — and on some benchmarks superior to — full-data tuning across three competition-level and six comprehensive math benchmarks. See also the OpenReview submission (ICLR 2026).

Why this matters. Long-CoT distillation is dominated by data quantity. Select2Reason shows that "what looks long and hard" is a surprisingly good selection criterion — and that naive scaling of synthetic CoT data is wasteful.

4.3 Impact of Fine-Tuning on Reasoning

Not all fine-tuning improves reasoning. The NAACL 2025 study "On the Impact of Fine-Tuning on Chain-of-Thought Reasoning" (arXiv:2411.15382) shows that while fine-tuning with RLHF, supervised fine-tuning, or QLoRA improves task accuracy, it may reduce the faithfulness of CoT reasoning. On average, faithfulness decreased across four datasets — meaning naive task-specific fine-tuning can harm reasoning quality.

Practical takeaway. If you fine-tune a reasoning model with task-specific data and don't preserve reasoning behaviour explicitly (via reasoning-aware data formats and evaluation), you may degrade exactly the capability you bought the reasoning model for. This is why Together AI's docs are emphatic: always include the reasoning field in your training data when fine-tuning a reasoning model.

5. Emerging Reasoning Models (2025–2026) #

Open-source reasoning models have flourished. The Clarifai "Top 10 Open-source Reasoning Models in 2026" blog notes that AI is shifting "from raw text generators to agents that act and reason," and that reasoning-first models — those which explicitly use internal deliberation or chain-of-thought — will power autonomous agents and strategic planners. Three highlights:

DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen3-8B

Released May 2025 as part of the DeepSeek-R1-0528 update. Distils reasoning patterns from the 671 B DeepSeek-R1 into Alibaba's Qwen3-8B base using ~800 000 high-quality reasoning samples focused on math, logic, and structured reasoning. Despite its small size, it is SOTA among open-source models on AIME 2024 (+10 % over Qwen3-8B), and after the R1-0528 update its AIME 2025 accuracy jumped from 70 % → 87.5 %.

Architecture identical to Qwen3-8B but with the DeepSeek-R1-0528 tokenizer config. Runs on a single 40–80 GB-VRAM GPU. MIT-licensed — supports commercial use and further distillation.

deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-R1-0528-Qwen3-8B · deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-R1

DeepSeek-V3.2 "Terminus"

A Mixture-of-Experts transformer using sparse attention for long contexts (up to ~1 M tokens) with ~37 B active parameters per token. Reinforcement training (GRPO) integrates math, coding, and tool use. Provides built-in thinking modes, function calling, and agent workflows. Tops open-source reasoning benchmarks but requires substantial compute.

Qwen3-Next-80B-A3B "Thinking"

Alibaba's next-generation open model with hybrid attention (Gated DeltaNet + Gated Attention) and a high-sparsity MoE with ~3 B active parameters out of 80 B total. The MoE uses 512 routing experts + 1 shared expert; each token activates 10 experts. Native context up to 262 144 tokens.

Benchmarks: ~87.8 % on AIME25 math, ~73.9 % on HMMT25 — outperforming earlier Qwen models and several proprietary systems. Stability tricks include zero-centered weight-decayed layernorm and Multi-Token Prediction.

Qwen/Qwen3-Next-80B-A3B-Thinking

Common pattern across the frontier

Combine large-scale reasoning data, efficient architectures (MoE, sparse attention), and specialised RFT (GRPO + verifiable rewards). The result: lightweight models that punch far above their parameter weight.

6. Practical Fine-Tuning: Step-by-Step #

Below is a practical workflow using Hugging Face Transformers and the PEFT package for LoRA. It trains a 7 B-class model on a chain-of-thought dataset (e.g., GSM8K). Adjust to suit your model and dataset.

from datasets import load_dataset
from transformers import (AutoTokenizer, AutoModelForCausalLM,
                          TrainingArguments, Trainer)
from peft import LoraConfig, get_peft_model

# 1. Load a base model pretrained for instruction following (Qwen, Mistral, ...)
model_name = "your_base_model"
tokenizer = AutoTokenizer.from_pretrained(model_name, use_fast=True)
model     = AutoModelForCausalLM.from_pretrained(model_name)

# 2. Load a CoT dataset. Each example needs `prompt`, `reasoning`, `content`.
dataset = load_dataset("gsm8k", "main")  # replace with your dataset

def preprocess(example):
    text = (f"Question: {example['question']}\n"
            f"Reasoning: {example['reasoning']}\n"
            f"Answer: {example['content']}")
    enc = tokenizer(text, truncation=True)
    example["input_ids"] = enc.input_ids
    example["labels"]    = enc.input_ids
    return example

dataset = dataset.map(preprocess, remove_columns=dataset.column_names["train"])

# 3. Configure LoRA
lora_config = LoraConfig(
    r=8,
    lora_alpha=16,
    target_modules=["q_proj", "v_proj"],   # adjust per architecture
    lora_dropout=0.05,
    bias="none",
)
model = get_peft_model(model, lora_config)
model.print_trainable_parameters()

# 4. Training
training_args = TrainingArguments(
    output_dir="./cot_finetuned_model",
    per_device_train_batch_size=2,
    gradient_accumulation_steps=8,
    num_train_epochs=3,
    learning_rate=5e-5,
    fp16=True,
    logging_steps=100,
    save_strategy="epoch",
)

trainer = Trainer(model=model, args=training_args, train_dataset=dataset["train"])
trainer.train()

# 5. Save adapter
model.save_pretrained("./cot_lora_adapter")

7. Tips and Considerations #

  • Dataset preparation. Ensure reasoning traces are high-quality and align with the answers. Remove noisy or contradictory CoTs using verifier models.
  • Instruction format. Use explicit delimiters (Reasoning: / Answer:) so the model learns to switch modes. Do not interleave the answer inside the reasoning.
  • Parameter-efficient fine-tuning. LoRA or QLoRA reduces memory and avoids overwriting the base model. DeepSeek and Qwen open models already support LoRA adapters.
  • Reinforcement fine-tuning. For further gains, consider DPO or GRPO after SFT. Reward models can be trained from preference data, but rule-based rewards suffice for math.
  • Evaluation. Use benchmarks like GSM8K, BBH, MMLUPro. Evaluate both final accuracy and reasoning faithfulness — for faithfulness, compare the generated CoT to known reasoning or prompt an LLM judge to verify.

8. Challenges and Future Directions #

  1. Unfaithfulness and error accumulation. Pre-thinking SFT often produces reasoning sequences that don't correctly lead to the answer. Research into mutual-information objectives and counterfactual training (e.g., FRODO) seeks to align answer with reasoning.
  2. Data efficiency. High-quality CoT data is expensive. Select2Reason shows that intelligent selection can match full-data performance with only 10 % of the data; more work is needed on active learning and curriculum design for reasoning.
  3. Resource-heavy reinforcement learning. RLHF and variants demand multiple models and large GPU budgets. Lighter methods (DPO, GRPO) are promising but still maturing.
  4. Long-context reasoning. Real tasks may need thousands of reasoning tokens. Sparse attention and MoE architectures (DeepSeek-V3.2, Qwen3-Next) help but remain costly.
  5. Evaluation of reasoning quality. Existing metrics focus on final answers; measuring the correctness and faithfulness of the reasoning trace itself is an open research problem. Process-level reward models and automatic critique LLMs are active areas.
  6. Safety and privacy. Reasoning traces may inadvertently reveal sensitive data or encourage harmful behaviours. Safe instruction tuning and content filtering are necessary.

The reasoning-first era. Fine-tuning for reasoning transforms surface-level text generators into thinking models capable of multi-step problem solving. New models like DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen3-8B prove that careful distillation and efficient fine-tuning can deliver high-quality reasoning at a fraction of the size of giant base models — while studies on faithfulness warn that how you fine-tune matters as much as what you fine-tune on.
