HOW TO USE THIS

A definition is a starting point, not an authority

Every entry below does three things: it says what the term means in this curriculum, it names the thing most commonly misunderstood about it, and it links to the source you should read when you need more than a sentence. The definitions are deliberately short and deliberately opinionated — a glossary that reads like a dictionary is one nobody returns to.

The links are the actual point. Each carries a label saying what kind of source it is, because the difference matters when you are deciding how much weight to give something: a specification and a well-argued blog post are both useful and they are not the same kind of claim.

Where my own recommendations live

Nowhere on this page. The definitions here describe what a term means and what its sources say; where this curriculum makes an architectural recommendation — layered by default with inversion where the rules live, boundaries enforced by a test rather than a network, keyset pagination over offset, an outbox for every dual write, CQRS only after three cheaper rungs — that recommendation is made in the relevant stage, with the reasoning attached and the alternatives argued. This page deliberately does not smuggle it into a definition.

THE PUBLISHERS

Where these links go, and when to reach for each

Fifteen stages cite a few hundred pages, and they come from perhaps twenty places. Knowing which place answers which kind of question is worth more than any individual bookmark — most of the time the problem is not that the answer is hard to find, it is that you looked in a source that was never going to have it.

One habit worth more than this whole page

When a fast-moving technology's behaviour matters to a decision you are making, check the current official documentation rather than trusting a definition — including these. Building this atlas turned up more than forty details that contradicted widely repeated claims: a Kafka producer setting that silently disables itself, a Spring listener that is skipped rather than degraded when no transaction is running, a Modulith feature that is opt-in rather than default, a widely attributed quotation nobody said. Every one of them was in the primary source, plainly stated, and absent from the secondary material. The habit is cheap and it compounds.

About this page. The definitions are written for this curriculum and are mine; the links are to primary sources and are labelled by kind — Official for a project's or vendor's own documentation, Standard for specifications (RFCs, W3C, WHATWG, Jakarta, OpenAPI, JEPs, OpenID), Reference for curated authorities that are not the vendor (MDN, web.dev, OWASP), Expert for named practitioners writing under their own name, and Research for empirical work. Where a term is contested or a source is disputed, the entry says so rather than choosing quietly. Links were checked against the sources cited across Stages 01–15 as those stages were built; documentation moves, and a dead link is a reason to search the publisher rather than to trust a cached copy.
