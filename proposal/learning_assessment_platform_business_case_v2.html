Goal-Oriented Learning Assessment and Guidance Platform

A product that tells a learner where they stand, which goal fits, and what to do next, with partners delivering the teaching. This version narrows the beachhead, treats the white space and prices as hypotheses, specifies how the assessment will be validated, and adds a bottom-up commercial model and launch gates.

Briefing scope (unchanged): commercial product; the assessment is the product; India first, then global. Changed in v2: the launch cohort is Classes 9 to 10 through school chains and CSR programmes. All ages, adult credentials, attention monitoring, state procurement and hardware move to later options, each behind a gate.

1. Executive summary

The product helps a learner understand their current position, choose a fitting goal, and take the most useful next action. The assessment gives the diagnosis, the guidance turns it into a decision, partner services deliver the action, and progress measurement gives the buyer a reason to return. The AI tutor is one of the actions, supplied by partners.

  • Who pays first: school chains and CSR education programme owners buying a per-student diagnostic and guidance licence for Classes 9 and 10, where a high-value decision (stream and subject choice) sits on top of visible academic gaps.
  • What decision the product improves: which stream and subjects a student should take after Class 10, and which gaps must close first. For the school: where to put remedial time and counselling hours.
  • What makes the buyer pay again: measured movement on the gaps the first assessment identified, plus the next cohort. Renewal is modelled, not assumed, and it is the central launch gate.
  • Why not "an AI tutor": explanations are moving to zero price (PhysicsWallah bundles its tutor under ₹5,000; Duolingo is folding Max features into cheaper tiers; Chegg's revenue halved). Diagnosis, placement and counselling still carry a price: ₹550 per Ei ASSET test, ₹2,400 for a Mindler report, ₹11,400 with counselling, $7 to $33 per student for US diagnose-and-instruct systems.
  • What is not yet proven: that the white space is real beyond the players studied, that budget holders will sign paid pilots at the hypothesised prices, and that the instrument is valid and fair. Section 10 makes further investment conditional on evidence for each.
Recommendation. Advance to a focused validation phase of about 12 months: assemble and calibrate the instrument with an assessment partner, run structured buyer interviews, and sign three to five paid pilots at three price points. Do not commit to India-wide rollout, state tenders, adult assessment, credentials or hardware until the gates in section 10 are met. Base-case year-3 revenue in the beachhead is about ₹8 crore (section 9); the case for the company rests on renewal and expansion from there, not on the first contract.

3.3 Assessment transactions, and why they are not "outcome-linked"

v1 grouped report fees, counselling bundles and certifications under "outcome-linked fees". They are assessment transactions: the buyer pays for a report or a service regardless of what happens next. Performance-linked contracts, where payment depends on a measured learning outcome, are a different instrument with attribution and incentive risks (who caused the gain, and the temptation to teach to the measure). The product may pursue performance-linked terms with CSR funders later; it is not part of the beachhead.

3.4 Hardware plus service

Benchmarks from v1 are retained for reference (Yoto £60 to 100 plus £8 to 10/mo; Osmo $79 plus app; Reachy Mini $399 to 499 with a non-commercial hardware licence; Moxie $799, cloud-only, bricked when the company closed in December 2024). 4. Competition, organised by the buyer's decision

Status of the white-space claim. Among the platforms studied, none combines academic diagnosis with a goal-oriented recommendation and a measured follow-through for Classes 9 to 10. That is a hypothesis about the market, not a proven gap: the universe of direct competitors has not been exhausted, and a buyer can already assemble assessment plus counselling from existing providers. The validation phase must test whether buyers see the difference and will pay for it.

8. Assessment validity: the real product risk

Deterministic scoring protects a score from model drift. It does not make the score true. A repeatable formula can still measure the wrong construct, disadvantage a group, or produce recommendations that do not hold up. Both engineering control and psychometric validation are required, and the product may not advise a learner on a dimension until that dimension has cleared the thresholds below. The framework follows the AERA, APA and NCME Standards for Educational and Psychological Testing.

8.3 Pilot size, derived from the study design

v1 named 2,000 to 5,000 students without justification. The number follows from three needs:

  • Item calibration: 500 to 1,000 responses per item for stable IRT parameters. With a bank of about 600 items delivered in overlapping forms, roughly 3,000 students suffice.
  • Reliability studies: a test-retest sub-sample of about 300 students and subgroup analyses needing at least 200 per group.
  • Outcome study: to detect a 0.2 SD effect at 5% significance and 80% power, about 394 students per arm are needed at the individual level. Students sit in classrooms, so with 30 students per class and an intraclass correlation of 0.15 the design effect is 1 + 29 × 0.15 = 5.35, giving about 2,100 per arm, or roughly 4,200 students in about 140 classrooms across 35 to 50 schools, half assessed-and-acted-on and half comparison.

So the validation phase needs about 4,000 to 4,500 students across the two uses, which is within the original range but now for a stated reason. Smaller paid pilots (section 9) run alongside for commercial learning; they are not the evidence base.

8.4 Who does this work

An assessment partner with Indian norming experience (Educational Initiatives is the obvious candidate and also the closest competitor; a university assessment unit is the alternative), an external psychometrician on retainer, a subject-expert panel per subject, and a safeguarding adviser for the counselling pathway. Budget: ₹1 to 1.5 crore for instruments, licences, expert fees and school incentives over the validation year (section 9).

9.4 Willingness-to-pay test (replaces fixed prices)

  • Structured interviews with 20 to 30 budget holders: chain academic heads, school principals who control fees, CSR programme owners. Record who signs, the procurement steps, the current spend on assessment and counselling, and objections.

10.2 Principles that do not change

  • Deterministic scoring. Scores and routing come from versioned, replayable rules and calibrated instruments. Models write explanations with citations back to computed values. A model change can never change a score.
  • Every assessment ends in an action. A remediation assignment, a teacher action, a counsellor referral or a partner course, with a date to re-measure. A report alone generates a transaction; a measured change gives the school a reason to renew.
  • Transparent where it builds trust, proprietary where it builds defensibility. Publish assessment purposes, rubrics, validation evidence, uncertainty handling and the rationale behind each recommendation. Keep item banks, calibration parameters, routing weights and partner workflows proprietary. Independent validation is a stronger moat than secrecy, but publishing calibrated items would destroy the instrument.
  • Human in the loop at decision boundaries. Low-confidence results and all stream recommendations pass through a trained counsellor before reaching a family.
  • Learner data protection by design. Purpose limitation per data field, role-based access, retention and deletion schedules, vendor contracts with processors, incident handling, and special protection for inferred attributes (section 11).

11. Regulatory position (corrected wording)

  • India, DPDP Act 2023 and Rules 2025. Section 9 requires verifiable parental consent before processing a child's personal data, prohibits processing likely to cause detrimental effect on a child's wellbeing, and prohibits tracking, behavioural monitoring and targeted advertising directed at children. The Rules exempt specified classes of data fiduciaries and specified purposes from parts of section 9, subject to conditions; educational institutions appear in that schedule for defined educational purposes. This is not a blanket exemption for any product sold through a school. The vendor's role (processor acting on the school's instructions, or fiduciary in its own right) and each processing activity need a specific legal assessment. For the beachhead, academic diagnostics and a counsellor-mediated recommendation are processed for the school's educational purpose; the attention and habits module is excluded precisely because it would be behavioural monitoring. Children's provisions of the Rules take effect about 18 months after notification (around May 2027). Penalties reach ₹200 crore for breaches of children's obligations and ₹250 crore for security safeguards.
  • Data location. Section 16 of the Act allows the government to restrict transfers to notified countries; the Act does not impose general data localisation. Hosting in India is chosen here as a procurement, security and trust decision that school and CSR buyers expect, not described as a statutory requirement.
  • United States, COPPA (amended rule effective June 2025, compliance by April 2026). Separate consent for third-party disclosure, retention limits, written security programme; schools may consent only for educational use. Relevant only when the product leaves India.
  • EU and UK. Digital consent age 16 by default (13 in the UK); the ICO Children's Code requires profiling off by default and no nudging. Relevant to later international options.
  • Sector conduct. The 2021 Ministry of Education advisory and the IAMAI India EdTech Consortium code (no auto-debit, no loan pushing, validated proof for outcome claims) apply to any family-facing sale. All efficacy statements in marketing must cite the validation studies in section 8.
  • Testing standards. The AERA, APA and NCME Standards are the reference for validity, reliability, fairness and intended use. India has no statutory psychometric standard; the Standards plus published evidence are the credibility bar for institutional and CSR buyers.

Before launch, the privacy design covers purpose limitation, parental verification where the school route does not apply, retention and deletion, role-based access, processor contracts, incident handling and protection of sensitive inferences (an aptitude profile is an inference about a child and is handled as such).
