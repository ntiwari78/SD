Product capability brief · 26 October 2026 · companion to the v2 business case

What the platform does, and why it is different

A goal-oriented learning assessment and guidance platform. It tells a learner where they stand, which goal fits, and what to do next; partners deliver the teaching; the platform measures whether it worked.

Scope follows the v2 business case: launch cohort is Classes 9 and 10 through school chains and CSR programmes, serving one decision (stream and subject choice after Class 10 and the gaps that block it). Items marked hypothesis are design intents to be validated in pilots, not demonstrated capabilities.

1. The idea in one page

Every platform studied is good at one of four jobs. Tutoring platforms explain. Adaptive systems diagnose within a subject. Counselling services recommend a path. Schools act. None of them joins the four into a loop for the individual learner, and none closes the loop by measuring whether the recommendation worked. This platform is that loop.

Figure 1. The platform owns the loop and the data that flows around it. It does not need to own the teaching: the Act stage is delivered by partners and the school.
The one sentence to validate: a single instrument that links a Class 9 or 10 student's academic gaps to a stream decision and then tracks whether the gaps closed. Everything else on this page exists somewhere already; the join and the follow-through are the claimed difference.

2. The learner journey (Class 9 student, school-licensed)

  1. Enrolment through the school The school enrols the cohort under its educational purpose; the platform receives an opaque learner ID, grade, board and medium. Identity stays with the school. Families receive a notice and a parent portal link.
  2. Baseline diagnostic, two sittings of 40 minutes Adaptive, board-aligned items in mathematics, science and language, reaching back to Class 7 and 8 prerequisites. Every wrong answer is tagged to a misconception, not just a topic. Delivered on school devices or a family phone; works offline and syncs later.
  3. Interest and reasoning profile, 25 minutes A short interest inventory and a reasoning battery, presented as exploration, not as a verdict. Low-confidence results are withheld and flagged for the counsellor.
  4. Goal conversation The student states what they are aiming at (a stream, a subject combination, an exam, or "not sure yet"). An AI assistant helps them articulate it in their own language; it does not score anything. If the student is unsure, the system proposes two or three plausible goals from the profile for the counsellor to discuss.
  5. Fit and gap report For each candidate goal: fit (interest and reasoning alignment), readiness (which prerequisite gaps block it, and how large they are), and effort (what closing them would take by Class 10 boards). Confidence bands on every number. The AI writes the explanation with citations to the computed values; it cannot change them.
  6. Counsellor review and family session Any stream recommendation passes through a trained counsellor (school staff or partner) before it reaches the family. The counsellor sees the same evidence and can override, with the override recorded.
  7. Action plan with a date A remediation set routed to a partner platform (PhysicsWallah, Khan Academy, Ei Mindspark or the school's own), a teacher action list for the class, and a re-test date 10 to 12 weeks out. The student sees three things to do this week, not thirty.
  8. Re-test and progress report The targeted gaps are re-measured. The school sees cohort movement; the family sees the student's movement; the decision is revisited if the evidence changed. The re-test is the next cycle's baseline.

Time cost per student per year: about three hours of assessment and two counsellor touchpoints. Time saved: the school stops running separate benchmark tests, counselling intake forms and remedial grouping by hand.

3. Inside the system

Figure 2. The guidance engine is the differentiating layer. The assessment engine must be excellent but is not unique. The foundation is what makes the product sellable to schools and programmes handling minors' data.
Assessment engine

Diagnoses with a confidence band, not a number

  • Adaptive items reaching back to prerequisite grades, so a Class 9 gap in algebra is traced to the Class 7 concept that caused it
  • Each distractor is tagged to a named misconception; the report says "treats negative signs as detachable", not "weak in algebra"
  • Deterministic scoring under item-response theory with a standard error on every score
  • Continuous psychometric monitoring: item drift, differential functioning by gender, board, medium and region
  • Offline delivery on low-end Android with later sync; printable paper forms with scan-to-score for schools without devices
Guidance engine

Turns a diagnosis into a decision the family can act on

  • A goal model: for each stream, subject combination and common exam, the prerequisite skills, typical effort, and the interest and reasoning profile that tends to fit hypothesis
  • Versioned routing rules: given gaps, profile and stated goal, produce fit, readiness and effort; every run is replayable and auditable
  • Decision boundaries: where a confidence band crosses a threshold, the system says "needs more evidence" and escalates to a counsellor instead of recommending
  • The language model writes the explanation in the family's language, cites the computed values, and is blocked from emitting scores or verdicts of its own
  • Counsellor override is first-class: recorded, reasoned, and fed back to improve the rules
Action and measurement

Closes the loop that nobody closes

  • Routes a remediation set to the partner the school already uses; the platform does not need its own content
  • Teacher action list per class: the five misconceptions shared by the most students this term
  • Re-test scheduled at plan creation; progress is measured on the targeted gaps, not on a generic score
  • Cohort and programme reports for chains and CSR funders: gap closure, decision stability, counsellor load
  • Outcome data accumulates into evidence of which actions work for which gaps hypothesis
Data and trust foundation

Built for minors' data from day one

  • Identity separated from behaviour: assessment and guidance services see an opaque learner ID; identity lives in an encrypted store the school controls
  • Purpose code on every data field and every model call; retention and deletion schedules by purpose
  • Append-only audit of scores, rule evaluations, overrides, prompts and responses
  • Tenant isolation at the database layer; programme funders get read scopes, never raw identity
  • Hosted in India for the Indian product; model inference inside the same boundary using open Indic models

7. What the platform deliberately will not do

Teach

No own content library, no own tutor. The Act stage is routed to partners and the school. Building content would put the platform in competition with its own channels and double the cost base.

Score with a language model

Models write and translate. Scores, fit, readiness and routing come from calibrated instruments and versioned rules. A model upgrade can change a sentence, never a number.

Monitor attention or behaviour

No keystroke, camera, engagement or habit tracking in the launch product. It is behavioural monitoring of minors under Indian law and a psychological assessment under testing standards; both need a different consent, instrument and safeguarding design.

Issue verdicts

No "you should become an engineer". Reports describe fit, readiness and effort with confidence; the decision belongs to the student, family and counsellor.

Recommend without a human at the boundary

Every stream recommendation and every low-confidence result passes a trained counsellor before the family sees it.

Sell to families directly at launch

Family reports are opt-in inside licensed schools. Direct consumer sale waits for school references and for the consent design the consumer route requires.

8. Trust, safety and evidence

ConcernDesign response
Is the diagnosis true?Instrument built and calibrated with an assessment partner; reliability, fairness and predictive validity thresholds must be met before a dimension is used to advise (business case, section 8). Evidence published.
Is it fair across groups?Differential item functioning checked by gender, board, medium of instruction and region; subgroup reliability reported; items failing are removed.
Can the AI make something up?The explanation layer receives computed values and a citation schema; outputs are checked against the values before display; approve and reject style verdict tokens are blocked; prompts and responses are logged.
Who sees a child's data?School-scoped identity; opaque IDs downstream; funders get aggregate scopes; role-based access; no third-party analytics in the student app; deletion on request and at programme end.
ConsentSchool-directed processing under the school's educational purpose where the exemption conditions are met; verifiable parental consent for anything outside it; the family portal shows what is held and why.
What if the result is uncertain?Standard error on every score; bands shown; results crossing a decision threshold are withheld and routed to a counsellor.
What if a recommendation is wrong?The re-test is scheduled at plan creation; the decision is revisited on evidence; counsellor overrides are recorded and analysed.
