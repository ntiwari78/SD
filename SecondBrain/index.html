How to Build Your Second Brain with plain folders and an AI

A step-by-step walkthrough of the "LLM Wiki" method: you collect raw material, a coding agent (Claude Code, Cursor, Codex…) compiles it into a personal wiki, and every question you ask makes the wiki smarter. No apps, no database — just three folders, one instruction file, and an AI.

Based on Nick Spisak's X article "How to Build Your Second Brain" (4 Apr 2026), which itself builds on Andrej Karpathy's LLM Wiki gist.

The idea in one picture

Think of it like a kitchen. raw/ is the grocery bag: everything you bring home, unwashed and unsorted. wiki/ is the pantry: the AI washes, labels, and shelves it so any ingredient can be found in seconds. outputs/ is the dinner table: the meals (answers, reports) you actually eat. The schema file is the recipe card pinned to the fridge that tells the AI how you like your pantry organized.

Collect → raw/▶ AI compiles → wiki/▶ You ask questions▶ Answers → outputs/ & back into wiki/↻
Why this beats "just search my notes" (RAG) With retrieval, the AI rediscovers everything from scratch on every question — nothing accumulates. With a wiki, the AI writes down what it learned once, links it, and refines it. Karpathy's phrase: the wiki is compiled knowledge, not raw files re-read each time.
1

Create three folders

~2 min

Open a terminal or file explorer, create a project folder anywhere, and add three subfolders:

my-knowledge-base/
  raw/          (your source material - articles, notes, screenshots)
  wiki/         (where your AI will write the organized version)
  outputs/      (answers, reports, and research your AI generates)

Or in one line: mkdir -p my-knowledge-base/{raw,wiki,outputs}

raw/

Your junk drawer of source material. You add here; the AI never edits here.

wiki/

Organized articles the AI writes and maintains. You read, you don't hand-edit.

outputs/

Answers to your questions, briefings, and reports.

No apps to install, no accounts to create. This is the same structure Karpathy uses.

2

Fill your raw folder

~10 min

This is where most people stall — they create the folders and stare at an empty raw/. The answer to "what do I put in?" is everything:

  • Copy-paste articles into .md or .txt files
  • Save screenshots and diagrams as images
  • Export notes from whatever app you use today
  • Meeting notes, research papers, project docs, PDFs
  • The bookmarks you've been hoarding for months
Don't organize it. Don't rename anything. Don't clean it up. Sorting is the AI's job. Your job is only to get material into the bag.
3

Automate collection with agent-browser

optional

Karpathy's post skipped the part that speeds this up: automated collection. agent-browser from Vercel Labs is a free CLI that lets your AI agent drive a real browser. Two commands to install (the second downloads a dedicated Chrome):

npm install -g agent-browser
agent-browser install

Then scraping a page into raw/ looks like this:

agent-browser open https://some-article-you-want.com
agent-browser get text "article"

Pipe that output into a file in raw/, or just tell your AI "scrape this URL and save it to raw/" and let it run the commands. It handles what copy-paste can't: JavaScript-heavy pages, pages behind a login, "load more" buttons, interactive figures. The article claims it uses far fewer tokens than Playwright MCP, so an agent can scrape several times more pages per session.

4

Write your schema file

~5 min

This is the step people skip. Don't. Create a file at the root of the project called CLAUDE.md (or AGENTS.md or README.md — the name doesn't matter, the content does). It is the AI's instruction manual for your knowledge base. Starter template:

# Knowledge Base Schema

## What This Is
A personal knowledge base about [YOUR TOPIC].

## How It's Organized
- raw/ contains unprocessed source material. Never modify these files.
- wiki/ contains the organized wiki. AI maintains this entirely.
- outputs/ contains generated reports, answers, and analyses.

## Wiki Rules
- Every topic gets its own .md file in wiki/
- Every wiki file starts with a one-paragraph summary
- Link related topics to each other using [[topic-name]] format
- Maintain an INDEX.md in wiki/ that lists every topic with a one-line description
- When new raw sources are added, update the relevant wiki articles

## My Interests
[List 3-5 things you want this knowledge base to focus on]

Karpathy keeps his "super simple and flat" in an AGENTS.md. No database, no plugin — a text file that states the rules. Add rules specific to you (e.g. "mirror the raw/ folder structure inside wiki/", "explain every topic with the Feynman technique", "end each article with Socratic review questions").

5

Tell your AI to compile the wiki

~15 min

Open Claude Code (or Cursor, Codex, any coding agent that can read your files), point it at the project folder, and say:

Read everything in raw/. Then compile a wiki in wiki/ following the rules
in CLAUDE.md. Create an INDEX.md first, then create one .md file per major
topic. Link related topics. Summarize every source.

Then step away and let it work. When it finishes you'll have a wiki/ full of organized articles, connections you didn't see, summaries of things you forgot you saved, and an index that makes everything findable in seconds.

The rule that keeps the system honest You don't edit the wiki by hand. You read it and ask questions against it; the AI keeps it updated. (Karpathy's tip: ingest sources one at a time with a quick human review of each summary, rather than one giant batch.)
6

Ask questions and save the answers

ongoing

Once the wiki has 10+ articles, start interrogating it:

  • "Based on everything in wiki/, what are the three biggest gaps in my understanding of [topic]?"
  • "Compare what source A says about [concept] vs source B. Where do they disagree?"
  • "Write me a 500-word briefing on [topic] using only what's in this knowledge base."

The AI answers across the whole wiki, grounded in your own material. Then save the answer back — drop it into outputs/ or have the AI fold the new insight into the relevant wiki article. Every question makes the next answer better. That's the compounding loop.

7

Run a health check

monthly

Compounding cuts both ways: if the AI writes something slightly wrong and you file it back, the next answer builds on the error. The fix is a periodic lint pass. Tell your AI:

Review the entire wiki/ directory. Flag any contradictions between articles.
Find topics mentioned but never explained. List any claims that aren't backed
by a source in raw/. Suggest 3 new articles that would fill gaps.

Karpathy's gist also suggests an append-only log.md in the wiki recording every ingest, query, and lint, so you (and the AI) can see how the knowledge base evolved.

8

You don't need Obsidian (but you can use it)

 

Half the replies to Karpathy's post pitched Obsidian plugins; Lex Fridman uses Obsidian + Cursor; startups are building dedicated tools. But the product is a folder of text files plus a schema. Open them in Claude Code, VS Code, Obsidian, or Notepad — the AI doesn't care. What matters is the folder structure and the schema.

The Notion trap Obsidian with 47 plugins means you spend more time configuring than using. Flat files and a good schema outperform a fancy tool stack most of the time. Stop shopping for the perfect tool; start building.

Where Obsidian does help: its Web Clipper for capturing pages into raw/, and graph view for seeing the [[links]] the AI created.
